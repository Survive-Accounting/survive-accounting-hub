## THE CORE STANCE

Students arrive panicked about a class famous for weeding people out. The
brand's whole posture, in five words:

> **"Meet the panic with a path."**

Not pacifying the panic. Not exploiting it. Meeting it — with a toolkit,
a pattern, a next step. And behind that, the operator's creed, the line
Lee wants "written on my chest":

> "We can't control how they respond. We can just do our best."

The emotional promise of the intro course: "All we're doing in this class
is interpreting business transactions — understanding how they impact a
pretty cool system called accounting — and it's all spoken in a language
you may not understand yet. Everything we're about to do is decoding
this." And the ambition: "Best case, I take you from a B to an A. If
you're just trying to pass — you're going to pass with flying colors."

## THE TEACHING LAWS

1. **QUESTION ORDER IS TEACHING ORDER.** The bank is not a pile; it's a
   sequence. Definition/recognition → Core → Application → Contrast/trap
   → Gray area/B→A. ~80% of a variant stays familiar; change ~20% so the
   student notices the tested distinction.
2. **Pretend you ARE the company.** The first move on every equation
   problem. Whose cash? Whose stock? You're Survive Co. (Canonical
   company: **Survive Co** — national bank stays generic; Oxford flavor
   lives in filmed examples only.)
3. **Lay up the answer. Give them the rainbow road to the answer.** The
   rubric sits at the bottom of the screen and questions climb it. Set
   the student up to score.
4. **Put a 12/31 button in your brain.** Train trigger-questions: WHEN is
   this happening? Which accounts changed? Why? The "when" button wins
   points on every adjusting-entries exam.
5. **Cash first = deferral. Recognition first = accrual.** Timing is one
   sentence, not a chapter.
6. **Balanced ≠ correct.** The trial-balance lesson, and quietly a life
   lesson about audits: "auditing isn't about accuracy, it's about
   fairness."
7. **Erase the misconceptions first.** Debits/credits get the Men In
   Black treatment: forget debit cards, forget credit cards — "it is just
   knowing how to increase or decrease these types of accounts. That's
   it." Then show THE FLIP — the holy-grail diagram: equity/revenue work
   the same because they share a goal ("we want to earn more; it makes us
   more valuable"), expenses press value down. The pattern, then the
   exceptions, then contra accounts.
8. **The word rent tells you almost nothing.** Prepaid Rent / Rent
   Expense / Rent Earned / Unearned Revenue — context decides everything.
   Keep confusing pairs TOGETHER on purpose: Equipment next to
   Accumulated Depreciation, Retained Earnings next to Dividends,
   Receivables next to Payables.
9. **Receivable = they owe us. Payable = we owe them.** Payables are
   ALWAYS liabilities. Prepaids are ALWAYS assets. Unearned Revenue: "you
   OWE the service."
10. **The Cash Cheat Code** opens journal entries: find cash first, then
    build the Tetris piece around it. "You see how it's sort of a Tetris
    piece?" Total debits = total credits, always.
11. **Challenge them honestly.** "The Blast Off is not always going to
    give you value gently — sometimes it's going to challenge you: hey,
    this is hard if you don't know how trial balances work. I'll explain
    them in the Vibe." Scary → but I'm here. That's the survival arc.
12. **Sing it when it helps.** Rhythm beats robotic speed. (Credit to
    Lee's buddy Jesús in Mexico, learning English by singing.) "We can't
    be robotic and we can't be too drawn out... we're going to have fun
    with it."

## THE PRODUCTION RHYTHM — BLAST OFF · JAM · VIBE

Locked names (Lee's call, Aug 2026): "We're calling them BLAST OFFS.
'Cram blast' is not even something someone would say."

- **BLAST OFF** — fast exposure. Lay the cards on the table, show what
  the exam problem looks like, show the mechanic/cheat code/pattern,
  drop the highest-value tips and traps. Grouped beats, never "problem 1,
  problem 2, problem 3" robotically. Ideal < 2 min; soft ceiling ~5 when
  the teaching earns it. Ends by sending the student forward — outros
  rotate, never ritualize.
- **JAM** — practice. Work the CEQs, repetition, pattern recognition,
  momentum. (Future Easter egg: the house-music Jam playlist.)
- **VIBE** — the deep pass. Slow down, explain why, return to the
  exhibit, gray areas, invite the student to talk back. Where trial
  balances get interesting and where Lee earns the A students.
- **NERD OUT** — social-first side format: one idea, casual voiceover,
  30–90s. Board-of-directors-is-both. Arthur Andersen/Enron. "The word
  rent tells you almost nothing." Allowance for doubtful accounts as a
  tease. Career ammo — students are in a job market.
- Student-facing mode labels stay **Cram / Practice / Full Review**
  during beta; Blast Off/Jam/Vibe is production language that may be
  tested student-facing later.

## KRUG, APPLIED TO TEACHING

**Don't make the student think** (about anything except the accounting):
right dose at the right time; one beat = one message; the next action
always obvious; never show database scale ("280 questions" is our
number, not theirs — show tonight's plan, time, progress); the exhibit
is a map, not decoration; the CEQ is a teaching beat, not merely
assessment.

## TALK — the active-learning primitive

At gray areas and traps, Lee says "tell me what you think" and the
player opens a talk/type popover → student explains → "See Lee's take →"
→ keep moving. No AI grading in V1. Why we ask: "talking it out forces
you to retrieve, organize, and commit — it converts the watcher into a
participant." Never punish the library kid: type is always equal.
Student questions are future content signals (with consent) — and asking
questions is brave: "a big part of Nerd Out content is giving you the
ammunition for great questions."

## THE FRANCHISE RECIPE (why this doc exists)

Survive is building a **franchisable teaching format** — the "For
Dummies" comparison: the content is valuable, but the FORMAT is the
asset. The recipe = this method + the phrase bank + the production
system (CEQs → exhibits → Blast Off/Jam/Vibe → shorts) + the growth
engine + Greek distribution ("every flyer in a Greek house is a
storefront"). A future Survive Finance tutor should be able to learn
this document, train in this method, and sound like themselves while
teaching the Survive way. Lee stays the face of the brand and the
keeper of the method: "Hey, I'm Lee from Survive Accounting — what's up,
Chad from Survive Finance."

Positioning has teeth: **proudly exclude**. "I'm working on the hardest
ones. If you took my Intro 1, you may not even need me for managerial —
that's how good I want Intro 1 to be."
