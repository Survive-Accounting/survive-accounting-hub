# Survive — the current style guide (exported 2026-09-06)

> **Superseded for illustrations (§1, §2, §6) on 2026-09-06 by `docs/ILLUSTRATION-STYLE-V5-PROPOSAL.md`, applied to the code the same night:** the house default is now `survive-riso` (riso inks, gold / blue / cream at 0.45 / 0.35 / 0.20), `survive-watercolor` v4 is kept as the style for the strategy shorts, and `BRIEF_SYSTEM` gained the surreality allowance, the enforced pair rule, the recurring student and the banned-subjects list.
> The rest of this guide — slides (§3), voice (§4, plus a fourth bio line, "I taught this course."), motion (§5) — is still current.

This is the house style **as the code actually applies it today**, pulled straight from the
files that generate pictures, draw slides and suggest lines. Nothing here is aspirational.
Workshop it, then bring back a new version; the last section says exactly which parts map to
which code so the new guide drops in without a rewrite.

Sources: `src/components/blastoff/illustration.ts` (the Recraft preset), `illustration-brief.ts`
(how a subject gets written), `canvas/cards/CalloutCard.tsx` + `blastoff/stage.ts` + `v3/Shell.tsx`
(colour and type), `brand-cards/bolt-boil.tsx` (the mark), `blastoff/canned-lines.ts` and
`rehearsal-brief.ts` (voice).

## 1. Illustrations — "Survive Watercolor" v4 (the house default)

Every picture is generated by Recraft V4.1 at 1024×1024 from **one sentence Lee brainstormed**
(the subject) wrapped in a fixed prefix and suffix (the style). The background is removed after
generation, so the picture sits on the black phone stage as a cut-out.

### 1a. The exact prompt the generator sends

**Prefix**

> A single illustration of

**Subject** — the brief's sentence (see §2), ≤ 70 words.

**Suffix** (verbatim — this is the style)

> , on a plain white background, filling most of the frame with only a small even margin around
> it. Bold editorial ink illustration: a clean, controlled hand-drawn outline in a warm dark
> brown — never black or near-black — with a watercolor fill that stays CONTAINED inside that
> line. No paint splatters, no muddy or uneven washes, no cloudy or bleeding backgrounds, no
> loose bleeding edges outside the outline — the wash is texture, not the whole picture. Every
> fill sits clearly between the two extremes: never true black or near-black (a suit, a shadow),
> and never white or near-white either (skin, a pale shirt) — a fill that pale is
> indistinguishable from the white background and gets cut away with it, leaving a hole. Skin
> and faces are always a warm tan, light brown, or warm peach, visibly darker than the white
> page. Subtle paper texture, high contrast, simplified shapes, a strong readable silhouette,
> one clear subject with minimal secondary objects and no unnecessary detail, at most two or
> three shapes total — designed to be immediately recognizable at small mobile-screen size, on
> screen for as little as two seconds. Vintage educational magazine illustration, modern
> composition. If it includes a person, show them from behind, from the side, with their head
> turned away, or cropped out of frame — never a detailed front-facing face. No text, no logos,
> no signature, no photorealism, no glossy cartoon shading, no clip-art look.

(When the subject carries a label — a sign that says OUR COMPANY — the "no text" clause is
dropped automatically.)

### 1b. Palette controls sent alongside the prompt

| Control | Value |
|---|---|
| Background | white `255,255,255` (stripped to transparent afterwards) |
| Accent 1 | Survive gold `252,163,17` (`#FCA311`), weight 0.35 |
| Accent 2 | Survive blue `0,107,166` (`#006BA6`), weight 0.30 |
| Model | `recraftv4_1` |
| Size | 1024 × 1024 |
| Motion on the slide | `drift` (a slow vertical hover, see §5) |

Weights must sum to ≤ 1 or Recraft rejects the call.

### 1c. The rules, and why each one exists

These were all earned on real generations. Keep the reason with the rule when you rewrite it.

| Rule | Why (what went wrong without it) |
|---|---|
| Outline is warm dark brown, never black | v1: a suited figure rendered dark-on-dark and vanished into the ground. |
| No fill is true black or near-black | Same defect; a black suit or shadow disappears. |
| No fill is white or near-white; skin is warm tan / light brown / warm peach | v2's "push away from black" landed skin on near-white. The background remover could not tell pale skin from the white page and cut the face out, leaving a black hole. Every fill must sit in a visible middle band. |
| Wash stays contained inside the line; no splatters, muddy washes, bleeding edges | v3 allowed "loose bleeding edges" and pictures read splotchy — uneven opacity, muddy faces, washed-out edges — and didn't survive two seconds at phone size. |
| One clear subject, two or three shapes, minimal secondary objects | Phone scannability. The picture is on screen for as little as two seconds. |
| Never a detailed front-facing face | Faces are where the model fails most visibly; back / side / turned away / cropped reads better. |
| No text unless the subject supplies one 1–3 word label | The generator's text is unreliable; a label is allowed only when it does real work. |
| Plain white background, small even margin | The cut-out needs a clean edge and the picture needs to fill the frame. |

**Version history:** v1 (2026-09-05) first watercolor after the monoline-on-black experiment
failed ("we need SOME colour"). v2 banned black fills. v3 banned near-white fills and named skin
tones. v4 (2026-09-06) contained the wash, added "one clear subject" and the phone-size line.
Any picture stamped with an older version shows "stale — regenerate" and regenerates from the
same subject.

### 1d. Retired: "Survive Dreamstate"

Monoline white pencil on solid black, no fill except one glowing shape. Kept only so old
pictures still render. Not the default and not coming back unless the workshop says so.

## 2. How a subject gets written (the brief)

Lee talks; the AI turns it into a title, three bullets and the subject sentence. The rules the
AI follows, verbatim from the code:

- Name **one concrete subject first** (earlier words weigh most). Describe structure and action,
  not adjectives — a pose, a prop, a direction of looking. At most three elements in the frame.
- A two-sided idea in one picture (inside vs outside, ours vs theirs, before vs after) uses a
  **single divider** — a wall, a window, a desk edge, a doorway — never two scenes side by side;
  the contrast is in where the figures are and what they hold, not in captions.
- A **pair across two slides** is two full single-subject pictures that match in rendering,
  world and cast (the second is briefed against the first as a reference and reuses its seed).
- **People, never a detailed front-facing face**: from behind, from the side, head turned away,
  cropped, mid-stride, climbing, at a desk seen from behind. A real, specific place or prop
  beats a generic one.
- **How old**: unless the idea needs someone senior, a person reads **young** — entry-level,
  about to graduate — said structurally (build, posture, a junior-looking role), not by age.
- **Colour mood** (more muted, warmer, colder) is stated in plain colour words when asked;
  never implied.
- **Text**: at most one short label of 1–3 words, only when it does real work, written as
  `with the words "OUR COMPANY" on the sign`.
- The brief **never** writes the style, medium, colours or background — the preset adds those.
- A **series** keeps the reference's cast exactly and changes only what the idea changes.

## 3. Slides — colour and type

### 3a. Colours

| Name | Hex | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Navy | `#14213D` | Detour card box, highlight text on gold, the deep ground |
| Cream | `#F5EFE6` | Ink on dark; the wordmark; the camera ring |
| Gold | `#FCA311` | Key phrase highlight, Cheat Code + Found On Your Exam accent, labels, buttons |
| Muted cream | `rgba(245,239,230,0.62)` | Secondary text |
| Edge | `rgba(245,239,230,0.16)` | Hairlines and borders |
| Bolt blue (lit) | `#006BA6` | The bolt's lit face; illustration accent 2 |
| Bolt blue (shade) | `#00456E` | The bolt's shaded face |
| Memorize orange | `#FF9F43` | Memorize This accent |

| Learn lime | `#E8FF47` | The hand-drawn portrait ink (parked) |
| Stage black | `#000000` | The phone stage behind everything |

Campus cards keep their own two school colours — that was decided and is not up for a darken.

### 3b. Type

| Role | Face | Size at card scale 1 | Weight / notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display (stems, key phrases, headings) | League Spartan | 31 | 800, line-height 1.1, letter-spacing −0.005em, balanced wrap |
| Body (bullets, lines) | Rubik | 19 | 500–600; nested bullets 16.5, muted |
| Labels / kind chips | League Spartan | 12 | 800, uppercase, letter-spacing 0.18–0.2em |
| Wordmark | League Spartan "surv⚡ve" | 5.2% of phone width as a watermark | cream; the bolt boils |

Fallbacks: `'League Spartan', 'Rubik', system-ui, sans-serif` and `'Rubik', system-ui, sans-serif`.

### 3c. The card anatomy

- **Set card** (a question): the canvas's 560-wide paper card, stem in display, choices below,
  gold highlight on the key phrase. Choices are lettered A–D; correct answers are now spread
  across positions.
- **Detour card** (Cheat Code, Memorize This, Deep Question, Found On Your Exam, Meet Your
  Tutor): the kind name **above** the box as a text-only chip in the kind's accent; the box is
  navy with a 1.5px accent border at 60%, 16-unit padding, 13-unit radius; key phrase
  highlighted gold-on-navy; bullets `›` (top level, 600) and `–` (nested, 500, muted).
- **Brand slides**: cold open (black, glow wordmark, tagline, campus ticker), intro (same
  wordmark position, topic name), bio (the tutor card), outro (fades; optional campus banner).
- **Stage**: 1080 × 1920. Safe zones: status bar top 9%, caption/title band bottom 20%, the
  like/share rail right 16% from 30% to 80% of the height. Cards sit top-aligned in the safe
  column at 470 × 1.24 (pass 2 template).
- **Camera**: a cream ring with a soft glow and a navy shadow, no nametag. Spots: home
  (bottom-left circle, 24% of width), corner (top-right, 22%), hero (big portrait top-centre,
  62%), top (centred circle, 34%), free. Ctrl+click = the "moment" (camera grows, slide dims).

## 4. Voice

### 4a. The canned lines (verbatim, these are the only ones)

**Intro** (also read over the cold open)
- Easy Points — "A lot of students I tutor miss this on the exam, and it's easy points. I'm Lee, and I want to help you with that."
- Ten Years — "I've tutored this course for ten years. These are questions students miss all the time — I want to help you not miss them."
- Boring But — "Most students think this is boring. It's also on your exam. I'll try to make it interesting, but mainly I want to make sure you get all the points."
- Memorize This — "If you've got an intro accounting exam coming up, here's what you need to memorize."

**Bio**
- "I've tutored this for 10 years."
- "I've tutored over 1000 students."
- "I love helping students in this course."

**Outro**
- Standard (weight 3) — "Hope this helped. Thanks for using Survive."
- More Like This — "That's it. A lot more like this at surviveaccounting.com."
- Exam 1 Free — "Exam 1 is completely free. Go get it — surviveaccounting.com."

The picker avoids the line used last time and warns when one is used in half of the last six.

### 4b. Written copy rules

- A real person talking. No emoji, no exclamation marks, no self-amazement, nothing
  performative.
- Contained detail views, never full-screen takeovers.
- Callouts are called callouts; the kinds are Cheat Code, Memorize This, Deep Question.

### 4c. Teleprompter lines (what the AI is told when it suggests one)

- One or two short spoken sentences, **≤ 30 words**, exactly how Lee would say it out loud.
- **Keep it Lee's**: his own phrasing wherever it already works; cut filler and false starts;
  never swap in fancier or more formal words than he used.
- **Grounded in the slide**: reads naturally right after seeing the slide's text, never a recap
  of the bullets.
- Short-form pacing: a few seconds out loud, one clear thread, not a paragraph.
- "What you said, cleaned" adds nothing; "Suggested" may bring in a sharper phrase from the
  original Talkthrough.

## 5. Motion

| Piece | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Illustration drift | Pure vertical hover, 4.2–5.2 s per cycle (seeded per picture), lift up to 5 px at full intensity. No sway. |
| Bolt boil | 8-frame hand-drawn boil on the wordmark's bolt; "calm boil" is the slower variant. |
| Neon kind label | One glow pulse per 2.75 s (rest 0–78%, peak at 10%), never a blink. |
| Detour spotlight glow | Soft LSD glow on `::after`, 3.4 s, so re-toggling never restarts the label. |
| Camera move | 480 ms with a slight overshoot; a shrink-in-place uses a plain ease. |
| Camera arrival | The boiling bolt fills the ring while the webcam negotiates; in hero mode the wordmark's own bolt stays hidden until the camera is live. |
| Intro → first slide | 900 ms ease-out fade in (film mode only; off with reduced motion). |
| Typewriter | Line-by-line, never word-split. |

## 6. What to bring back, and where it goes

| If the workshop changes… | It maps to… |
|---|---|
| The look of the pictures (medium, line, fill, texture) | `illustration.ts` → `promptSuffix` of `survive-watercolor`, and a **version bump** (5) so old pictures show stale |
| The accent colours the generator reaches for | `illustration.ts` → `controls.colors` (keep weights ≤ 1 total) |
| Any new "never" rule | Add it to the suffix **with the reason** in the comment above it — the black/near-white rules are empirical, not taste |
| How subjects are chosen (people, age, labels, dividers) | `illustration-brief.ts` → `BRIEF_SYSTEM` |
| A second house style (e.g. for the strategy shorts) | A new entry in `ILLUSTRATION_STYLES` with its own id; keep watercolor as `DEFAULT_STYLE_ID` unless you say otherwise |
| Slide colours | `CalloutCard.tsx` `DETOUR` / `detourAccent`, `v3/Shell.tsx` `V3_*` |
| Type | `stage.ts` `DISPLAY_FONT` / `BRAND_FONT`, `CalloutCard.tsx` sizes |
| The canned lines | `canned-lines.ts` `CANNED_LINES` (text, slot, weight) |
| How suggested lines sound | `rehearsal-brief.ts` `REHEARSAL_SYSTEM` |
| Motion timings | `DriftBoil.tsx`, `CeqPreviewer.tsx` `PV_CSS`, `Webcam.tsx` |

Questions worth answering in the workshop:

1. Is "vintage educational magazine, modern composition" still the reference, or is there a
   specific artist / era / print process to name instead?
2. Should the gold and blue accents stay, or does the picture palette want a third colour?
3. Do strategy shorts (reps, chairs, founder notes) get the same picture style or their own?
4. Are there subjects that should be banned outright (money piles, handshakes, lightbulbs)?
5. Is the no-face rule permanent, or is a consistent recurring character wanted?
6. Do any canned lines retire, and does the bio want a fourth option?
