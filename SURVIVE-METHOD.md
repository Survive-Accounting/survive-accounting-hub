# The Survive Method
The teaching canon for Survive Accounting. Everything filmed, built, or licensed
follows this document. The production standard (the Survive Format) lives at the
bottom and will grow separately.
## The Law
Ask first. Never explain before an attempt or an explicit skip.
Questions reveal where the student is; explanations land only after.
This applies to videos, exhibits, CEQs, and live tutoring.
## The Rubric
A = L + E  |  Revs & Exps
- Left of the pipe: the balance sheet universe. Right: the income statement.
- The Retained Earnings statement is the bridge.
- Signs: (+/−) left of the equals sign; (−/+) on L + E. The flip is the point.
- Revenue lives with Equity (earning increases equity).
- Expenses are Equity's opposite (they reduce NI → R/E → Equity).
## The Probes
The ten reusable question moves. Any transaction × any probe = a teachable moment.
1. The Four Questions — type? which account? increase/decrease? anything else?
2. Rewind — what entry came before this?
3. Fast-Forward — what entry comes next?
4. Statement Check — which statement(s), and how presented?
5. Year-End Cross — we crossed year-end; what adjusting entry?
6. Accrual or Deferral? — which, and why?
7. Date Check — what date, and what does it change?
8. What If We Don't? — skip the entry; what's over/understated?
9. Show Me the Math — compute the amount.
10. Flip It — mirror the timing/side; what changes?
## Cheat Codes
Named shortcuts. A cheat code is always legitimate method, never a way around
learning — the word means "shortcut," and copy must never drift toward
beating-the-system framing.
- The Cash Cheat Code: if cash is involved, the cash side of the entry takes two
  seconds — cash received is a debit to Cash, cash paid is a credit to Cash.
  Solve the cash side first, then reason about the other side.
- The Four-Question Cheat Code: the walkthrough above, run fast.

## Exhibits
Interactive, progressively revealed teaching artifacts. Rules:
- Built for filming first; student interaction is the same mechanism, exposed later.
- Reveal steps narrow the universe as the student commits (the zoom).
- Content is generic to any campus — no school names, professor names, or dates
  inside an exhibit.
## The Survive Format (production standard — grows later)
- Note frames open with FOUND ON YOUR EXAM and the quoted question.
- No dates, schools, or professor names in filmed pixels; the player stamps context.
- One take discipline; signal phrases mark exam-critical moments.
